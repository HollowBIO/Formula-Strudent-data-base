# Engine Decision – Honda Transalp XL700V vs Yamaha MT-07 (CP2)

## Decision

- Plan A: use the team's existing Honda Transalp XL700V engine (680 cc V-twin)
- Condition unknown: it has been sitting on a wooden plate for about 6 years
- Go criteria: running within **a few weekends** and for **under about $1,000**
- If it misses either, fall back to the Yamaha CP2 (689 cc parallel twin)

## Plan A – Transalp revival checklist

- Turn it by hand with the plugs out (if it's seized, suspect cylinder rust)
- Drain the oil and check it (milky means water got in)
- Compression test and leak-down test
- Refresh: oil, coolant, plugs, dried seals
- Missing because it's engine-only: harness, ECU, injectors, fuel pump, throttle bodies
- Rough cost: about $100–300 refresh, $200–500 used ancillaries, plus ECU

## ECU options (either engine)

- DIY Speeduino: about $200–300
- Ecumaster EMU Black class: about $1,000

## Plan B – Yamaha CP2 donor bikes (same 689 cc engine family)

- Yamaha MT-07 (most listings in Ukraine)
- Yamaha XSR700
- Yamaha Tracer 7 / Tracer 700
- Yamaha Ténéré 700
- Yamaha R7
- A crashed donor bike or engine on its own is the cheapest route

## Why the Yamaha may be the better new engine

- Proven in Formula Student (e.g. FS Oulu M03: about 75 hp, car about 200 kg)
- Much better availability in Ukraine: about 50–85 MT-07 listings vs about 10 Transalps
- Lighter, more modern engine with a bigger aftermarket
- More power on paper (about 73 hp stock vs about 60 hp) and a higher rev limit (about 10,000 vs about 8,500–9,000 rpm)
- Easier to find spares and a replacement engine during the season

## Trade-offs to remember

- Transalp: flatter low-end torque, less affected by the 20 mm restrictor (about 0.047 vs 0.056–0.062 kg/s of air needed at peak power; the restrictor chokes at about 0.07 kg/s)
- Both are capped at roughly 75–80 hp by the restrictor
- The 710 cc rule limit rules out bigger twins (KTM 790/890)

## Cooling – split radiators (Yamaha)

The stock MT-07 has a single front radiator with one fan; splitting it into two sidepod radiators is mostly plumbing.

- Y-split the hot line from the engine to a radiator in each sidepod, and rejoin the returns before the pump
- Run them in parallel, with equal-length hoses for an even flow split
- Put a bleed point at the highest spot to avoid trapped air
- One fan per radiator
- The stock water pump is usually enough; about an afternoon of fabrication

## Cost estimates (rough)

- Existing Transalp: about $500–1,800 total depending on the ECU
- Used Transalp bike: from about $4,700, so about $5,000–5,700 with ECU
- Used MT-07 bike: about $6,350 (2015, 25,000 km example), so about $6,600–7,400 with ECU
- Engine-only or crashed MT-07 donor: about $1,500–2,500 (estimate)
- Selling unused bike parts gets back part of the cost
