# Noise Test and Silencer

The car must stay under 110 dB(C) up to the test speed and 103 dB(C) at idle; a cheap used slip-on silencer plus our own sound-meter testing is the starting plan.

## Rules (confirmed from inspection sheets and rules excerpts)

- Idle: max 103 dB(C), fast weighting
- Test speed: max 110 dB(C), fast weighting, at all speeds up to the test speed
- Test speed = rpm at 15.25 m/s mean piston speed, rounded to the nearest 500 rpm
- Static test, gearbox in neutral
- Microphone level with the outlet, 0.5 m away, at 45°; every outlet checked
- Exhaust outlet no more than 45 cm behind the rear axle

## Test speed for our engines (bore/stroke from memory — verify)

| Engine | Stroke | Test speed | Share of peak-power rpm |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Honda Transalp XL700V | 66 mm | about 7,000 rpm | about 90% |
| Yamaha CP2 (MT-07) | about 68.6 mm | about 6,500 rpm | about 70% |

The Transalp is likely slightly harder to quieten (tested closer to full power), but the silencer decides it; not a factor in engine choice.

## Silencer options

- Reuse or buy a motorcycle silencer: stock Transalp is quiet but about 4–6 kg; a used aftermarket slip-on (Akrapovič, Leo Vince, etc.) is about 2 kg
- Make our own: straight-through absorptive design — perforated stainless tube, basalt or fibreglass packing, light shell, end caps; repack every season
- Used silencer from another FS team: already sized for a restricted engine

## Plan

- [ ] Get a cheap used slip-on for early testing
- [ ] Borrow or buy a sound meter (dB(C), fast weighting)
- [ ] Measure at idle and at the test speed, at 0.5 m and 45°
- [ ] Well under 110 dB(C): keep it or build a lighter one; close to the limit: make it longer or add packing
